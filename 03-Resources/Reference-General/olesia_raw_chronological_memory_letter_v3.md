*A letter to myself, and to whoever needs to understand — not a legal document, just the truth as I lived it.*

---

I got married on 28 July 2010. In those days we lived in the center of Moscow — first on Moscow River Embankment (Smolenskaya str, 10) with a view of the Kievsky bridge across the Moscow River, carrying traffic from Noviy Arbat to Kutuzovskiy Prospekt (one of the main governmental and traffic arteries in Moscow). Besides the convenience of our house's location (private parking, public transport, high safety standards — the building was situated right at the corner of the British Embassy), both Eugene and I simply fell in love with the stunning view from the panoramic windows of the flat (pics attached), the moment I found it. This is actually how we met — a couple of years before the marriage, I was working as a Business Developer and Real Estate Manager for a company called Crown International, and we contributed to a relocation package deal with Wavin Rus (a Russian subsidiary of Wavin B.V., the Dutch plastic pipe manufacturer, one of the largest companies in Europe in this industry). Eugene Rasing was appointed Managing Director of Wavin Rus and needed our full package of relocation services (including international moving, document support, house search, and settling-in assistance).

At some point we moved to Rochdel'skaya street, building 5, right in front of the White House on one side of the apartment, and the beloved Moscow River view on the other. Yeah, we kind of always enjoyed beautiful views from our windows and could wind down well on the balcony there, feeling ourselves sort of on top of the world… :-) Later we moved to Baumanskaya street. Less prestigious positioning for the spoiled Moscow-heart living people we were, but still right in the historic center. We were always in an elevated ("expensive") part of the city. But most importantly, I was happy about this move, as it was finally our home — one I managed to finish the fit-out of right after our daughter was born. I'd rented a new office and warehouse right across from our new flat and was very glad to continue being actively, daily present running my business while being a young mum. That was our life then.

By that point I had already built something of my own — a garment retail business, from literally nothing, up to 8 physical shops and somewhere between €1 and €1.5 million a year in revenue when it grew. It wasn't handed to me — Eugene helped me start it up, but it was me who built it.

In August 2017 we moved to Florence as a family. Not because I wanted to — I actually didn't want to relocate to Italy, precisely because I knew my "baby-project," my company, wouldn't survive without me physically there running it. But we moved anyway. Family first.

For the first year I kept flying back and forth between Firenze and Moscow. Partly it was for the visa regime — I still held a long-term visa and had to stay within the 180/180-day rule. But mostly it was because I still cared, deeply, about my little business I had built. I applied for my residence permit on the grounds of family reunification somewhere in 2019–2020, and by 2020 I was barely travelling, and then (around 2021) had stopped travelling internationally altogether. The years of back-and-forth in between — that was the beginning of the end for me (and maybe for us as a couple), in more than one way.

I believe it was hard for Eugene to manage the household even with the nanny there. Then it became nearly impossible when she needed to go back to Russia (she was Russian, our nanny, whom we'd brought with us from Moscow). Well, I kind of get it — we had nobody in Italy besides ourselves, no parents, no siblings, not even friends when we moved in. And the kids were still so little, they needed their mummy constantly, not a mother who reappears every few weeks. So after a year of tearing myself apart between two continents, I made the decision — I closed a couple of shops. Then gradually the biggest part of the shops was gone. I didn't want to keep flying away from my family for business trips and stay away for too long, and I realized retail at that scale simply cannot survive without the founder physically present.

For a while I kept a small piece of it still alive — two shops plus some online (e-commerce attempts). I tried, for a couple of years, really… watching it slowly fade: the company was opened in 2010, we moved in 2017, by 2021–2022 it was barely breathing, and in 2022–2023 (delisting procedure) it was officially closed and delisted.

At some point (February 2022), my daughter ended up in the hospital with a life-threatening illness. Needless to say, everything else stopped mattering. I was with her 24/7 for the duration of the hospitalization and the months of recovery afterward. Whatever tiny bits of my professional life were still hanging on — I let them die. There was no other choice, and honestly, I wouldn't have made a different one, would I?

So yes — all in all, I sacrificed my career, and practically my own sense of purpose, the project I had cared about so much, for my family. I knew this would happen before we even left Moscow. That's exactly why I didn't want to move.

Moving my savings out afterward wasn't simple either — SWIFT was blocked between Russia and Europe, so at some point crypto rails were the only option left. And I lived off those savings. I never once asked my husband for financial help. He covered the rent fully — starting from the last months in Firenze (before that, we split it evenly), then in Lerici, then in Genova. But everything else — the children's activities, their day-to-day life, everything a mother does — that was on me, from my own money.

Eventually, most of my money ran out. I had "eaten it up," as I put it to myself. And while I was scraping by, the tension between me and my husband kept growing. At some point he bought himself a new BMW X5 M — I heard estimates of €100,000 to €110,000 — instead of keeping the one I had given him for his birthday back in Moscow (a previous version of the BMW X5 M). He bought an IWC watch (something like €22,000–23,000). He went on trips to good-to-premium hotels, ate at expensive restaurants. "It doesn't matter how much we spend, what matters is how much we make," he used to say. Right… Back in the day (in Moscow), it was actually me who invented this phrase. I was kidding… ehh, sort of… was I? :-) Anyway, he used to see me this way — I would always stand up for myself, never asking him for anything. If he wanted, he would treat me to something nice, but neither of us was used to a situation where I would need to ask him for money…

Needless to say, I was emotionally affected by the fact that he did not see (or did not want to admit) how the situation had changed; nor was he seeing me as a person, or hearing me out anymore. As if I'd vanished… only the utility stayed. All that was good between us as a couple disappeared; the parental duty stayed. While he was frequently away on his business trips, enjoying his business grow (he'd opened his own company, producing test equipment for plastic pipe manufacturers), I stayed at home with the kids… and with my new friend — a growing anxiety (about money and my not-yet-ruined career prospects) — accompanied by another friend: a strong feeling of a lack of appreciation. I was driving an old, worn-out Mercedes that had been through years of family trips and business errands (Eugene used it too for quite a while — a couple of times he hauled his own work equipment around Europe in it, a car that wasn't assigned to him for that, but at the time it didn't matter), figuring out, every single month, how to cover the fundamentals when he was away.

While he worked full time and traveled constantly, I was fully focused on our children and our household. My tiny, unfocused attempts to restart something professional were doomed from the start — and in a small, conservative, rather cut-off place like Lerici, with no direct train connection, I don't think any "fashion genius" could dust themselves off and rebuild a business from scratch, not really. I understood that with my mind. But with my heart, I felt what he was really saying — that this was somehow my own fault, that I was "always too emotional, this is why I failed," and so, "as it was all my own fault, I should not make a drama out of it."

I told him his lack of empathy had turned into something closer to real cruelty (I'll leave further details out, so as not to drag myself down mentally with these records). I started trying to leave. We talked about separation, on and off, over and over. Every time I felt sure of my decision, he would talk me out of it, promising me that his attitude toward me and things for me would change. At least I managed to talk him into moving to Genova, holding him to his word on multiple promises. Oh well, it was rather a coincidence working in my favor, too — the landlord claimed the rented house back, so we needed to move quickly, regardless of Eugene's attempts to drag things out.

In Genova, after I'd settled all the organizational affairs (house furniture and other arrangements, a new school in the middle of the scholastic year, medical care, sports, new social connections, and the rest for the kids and the family overall), I thought I could finally breathe out for a while… Have I not deserved just a little mental break? I was rather quickly reminded that I needed to start looking for a job, to "stop sitting on his neck" and "stop playing Bitcoin queen"…

What can I say? The location is not the solution to the problem. One cannot run away forever…

I tried to compromise with myself, distracting myself with social media (a kind of self-invented, synthesized substitute for appreciation). He grew jealous, started following my account, broke it, started stalking me and the people there. We even went to family therapy after it all burst. It didn't take us very far — besides the promises, there still wasn't much moral or any other kind of support coming from his side — plenty of criticism, I felt. He eventually didn't want to continue the sessions, blaming the therapist herself, accusing her of pushing me toward wanting a divorce — when really, I think I became more convinced of that because of how he behaved even during the sessions. Aggressive, even there. Once he yelled at her, so she stopped the session.

The dissatisfaction on both sides reached its limit. So from my side — I am declaring that living under one roof is no longer possible. I am asking to live with my children, with their father seeing them as is normally arranged, and I won't stand in the way of that. But I am not, right now, in a position to cover all the running costs of life for my children and myself alone. So I have to ask him for a financial contribution — enough to maintain the decent standard of living they're used to, and let the kids keep doing their social and sports activities, with me there to bring them.
