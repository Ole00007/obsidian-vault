# Children-first strategies - proposal for Alessia and the avvocato (for discussion as a team)

Date: 05 11 2026. Companion to: "Consolidated summary - Alessia vs Eugene - 05 11 2026.md" (strategies A, A2, B, C, D). This file adds two strategies, E and F, plus a toolkit. Not legal advice and not a clinical opinion: every point is for the avvocato and a child psychologist to confirm.

## 0. Context in one paragraph
Two children of sensitive ages: a boy (10) in the last year of primary school, preparing for scuole medie and training actively in a professional football school; a girl (almost 13) in the last year of scuole medie, preparing for liceo, who has just restarted sport after a break of more than a year caused by a sport-related trauma. Alessia stayed in an unhappy marriage for years for the children and wants their daily lives and progress affected as little as possible by the separation. She does not intend to tell them that she does not love their father. Items to confirm are marked [CONFIRM].

## 1. What the sources say about children in separation (short)
- Children adjust better when they have continuity, predictability and consistent routines, and when conflict is kept away from them [1].
- Children say they want their parents to end the conflict and be civil and respectful to each other [2].
- In Italy, shared custody means both parents agree on schooling, health and leisure activities, and extraordinary expenses (for example costly sport activities) are split in proportion to income [3].
- Courts decide on school and activity changes by the child's interest, not by a parent's convenience; one tribunal refused a school transfer justified only by the mother's convenience [4].
- A tribunal has also preferred keeping children in the family home with the parents alternating, to protect established life habits (one decree; not a general rule) [5].
- Hearing of the child: in judicial separation the judge must hear a child aged 12 or older, and younger if capable of discernment; in consensual proceedings it is left to the judge's discretion [6][7]. The girl is about to turn 13, and the boy (10) could be heard if judged capable of discernment [CONFIRM with avvocato].

## 2. Strategy E - "Children's Stability Plan" (lead the negotiation with the children, not the couple)
Idea: put one written parenting plan at the front of every negotiation, before house and money, built around what must NOT change.

What must stay the same (the "protected list"):
1. School for both children until the end of the current school year, then the planned transitions (media for the boy, liceo for the girl).
2. The boy's football school: same club, same training days, same travel.
3. The girl's restart in sport: same activity, coach and schedule; no big change in the first months of her return.
4. Friends, neighbourhood and main routines (meals, bedtime, homework).
5. A calm, predictable relationship with both parents.

What goes into the written plan (proposals for the avvocato to turn into clauses):
| # | Clause | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Shared custody with a clear primary residence and a weekly calendar | Predictability for the children |
| 2 | Continuity clause: school, sport club and coach do not change unless both parents agree in writing or the judge decides | Protects progress and the girl's sport restart |
| 3 | Transport and logistics table for training, matches and school, with a back-up person for each slot | No missed sessions; no conflict at the pitch |
| 4 | Sport costs and equipment as extraordinary expenses split in proportion to income [3] | Removes a money argument that touches the children |
| 5 | "Neutral ground" rule: no discussion of the separation, money or lawyers at school, training or matches; no arguing in front of coaches | Protects the boy's club and the girl's re-entry |
| 6 | Children are never messengers or witnesses: no questions about the other parent, no showing messages, recordings or documents to them | Avoids loyalty conflicts |
| 7 | Same-message rule: one agreed short announcement to the children (see 5), no separate versions | Avoids blame stories |
| 8 | Holiday and birthday calendar, including the grandparents on both sides | Keeps the extended family connected |
| 9 | Review every 3 months with a mediator or psychologist, and a mediation step before any court step | Adjusts to the children's real reaction |
| 10 | Child-support channel: a named psychologist or school counsellor available to each child | Early warning |

Why it fits this case: Eugene's messages often use "the children" and "the family" as guilt arguments (see the consolidated summary). A plan focused on concrete child routines moves the conversation from blame to logistics, and his written willingness to seek an amicable solution can be tied to it.

Risks and mitigation: he wrote that he could organise an apartment "for myself and the kids". The residence and calendar must therefore be agreed in writing early, and the avvocato should consider an interim agreement or an interim court measure if he will not sign.

## 3. Strategy F - "Staged separation": from a cold war at home to two stable homes
Idea: do not wait for the whole separation to be finished to reduce the stress at home, and do not move the children abruptly. Move in stages tied to the school and sport calendar.

| Stage | When | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| F0 - Calm rules under one roof | Now | A written "house rules" page for both parents: no arguing in the children's presence or hearing, separate spaces and routines where possible, communication only by short written messages about logistics, a fixed time once a week for adult topics (or via the avvocato/mediator). The goal is to stop the cold-to-hot war the children see, even before the final agreement. |
| F1 - Interim written agreement | In the next weeks | Parenting calendar, school and sport continuity, money for household and children, who stays where, and a target move date. Signed through the avvocati. |
| F2 - Move at a child-friendly moment | Chosen with the school and sport calendar [CONFIRM dates] | For example: not in the middle of the girl's final school-year exams period or of the boy's key matches; ideally a break in the school year or the summer. A temporary solution is acceptable if it ends the cold war earlier. |
| F3 - Review | 3 months after the move | Check-in with the mediator or psychologist, adjust the calendar. |

Three ways to organise F2 (the avvocato should assess which one is realistic and legally sound):
| Option | What it means | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|---|
| F-a Two homes close to school and football school | Alessia and the children move to a rented home within the same school and sport area; Eugene gets a fixed weekday and weekend schedule | Same school, same club, same friends; clear separation of the parents | Needs a rental, deposit and money agreement; Eugene may resist |
| F-b "Nesting" for a limited time | Children stay in the family apartment and the parents alternate living there; each has another place on their days off | Maximum stability for the children [5] | Costly; needs low conflict and a time limit; difficult if tensions remain high |
| F-c One parent leaves first | Either parent leaves the apartment; the children keep their residence in the apartment | Quick end of the shared tension | Who leaves, and what it means for the lease and future custody, must be decided with the avvocato; may be seen as abandonment if poorly documented |

Note on the lease: the apartment is rented (he wrote about taking over the rent or cancelling the contract). The lease name, the end date and the notice period decide how fast any option can happen [CONFIRM].

## 4. Toolkit for protecting the children (supports E and F)
1. **How to tell the children.** One short, calm, agreed message, ideally with both parents or with a psychologist present, for both children the same day. Content: the parents are separating because they will be happier in two homes; it is not the children's fault; both parents will always be their parents; what stays the same (school, club, friends, family); what changes (where they sleep, the calendar); they can ask questions and feel any feeling. No details about blame or about love. Alessia does not need to say she does not love their father.
2. **Age-specific notes.** The girl (nearly 13) will probably want some say and could be heard by the judge in a judicial case; give her information and choices without making her decide. The boy (10) needs clear routines and reassurance that football continues; avoid asking him to choose between parents.
3. **Sport.** Tell the coaches only what is needed (for example, a calendar change) without details. For the girl's return to sport after a trauma, ask her sports psychologist, GP or the club's staff whether a gradual plan is advisable; protect training times from schedule changes.
4. **School.** Inform the school counsellor or the class teachers of the main facts, in a neutral way, so they can watch for changes in mood or grades. Both parents are informed of school choices; the choice of liceo and of scuola media is a joint decision [3][4] [CONFIRM enrolment dates with the school].
5. **Child psychologist or school counsellor.** One neutral professional for the children (not the one used by the parents), available for both. ASL3 in Genoa has a consultorio for family counselling [see consolidated summary].
6. **What to avoid.** Arguing in the children's presence; the cold war at the dinner table; asking the children to report on the other parent; leaving recordings, messages or lawyers' papers where they can see them; negative comments about the other parent; using the children as a reason for guilt, in either direction.
7. **Warning signs to act on early.** Sleep or appetite changes, drop in school results, withdrawal from football or from the girl's sport, stomach aches, anger, constant checking of parents' phones. Talk to the school counsellor or paediatrician.

## 5. How to bring E and F into the negotiation (for the avvocato)
1. Open the written proposal to Eugene with the children's protected list and the 10 clauses (Strategy E), before house and money.
2. Offer F0 immediately, as a low-risk first step he can accept without "losing" anything.
3. Offer a mediator or psychologist for the review and for the message to the children.
4. Ask for a reply with dates (7-14 days). If there is no reply, escalate to the judicial track prepared in parallel (Strategy B) and consider asking for interim measures on residence, calendar and school/sport continuity.
5. If the girl is to be heard (judicial case), the avvocato should prepare how this will be handled so that neither parent influences her; the same applies to the boy if heard.

## 6. Open questions for Alessia (needed to finalise)
1. Where do the children live and go to school now; where is the football school; how far is each from where you might live?
2. What was the girl's sport and how is the restart going (days, coach, location)?
3. Who currently takes the children to training and school, and what is Eugene's travel pattern?
4. Do the children already know about the separation, or sense it?
5. Lease: whose name, end date, notice period?
6. What are the key dates of this school year (exams, enrolment, matches)?
