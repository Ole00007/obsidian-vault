# Blueprints and Evidence

## What successful traders actually teach (process, not return claims)
- Mike Bellafiore (SMB): a "good trade" follows preparation, risk control, and execution regardless of outcome; daily risk limits and surviving losses come first.
- Linda Raschke: start with a very small account because losses are tuition; have a daily game plan covering adds, stop moves, and partial profits.
- Ed Seykota (public summaries): survival first, small fixed-fraction risk (often cited 1-2% or less), cut losses, trend-following with volatility-based stops; whipsaws in sideways markets are a known cost.
- Paul Tudor Jones (public summaries): defence first, define invalidation before sizing, skip trades when setups are absent, never average down.
These are secondary summaries of public statements; treat as hypotheses to test, not audited strategies.

## Why overtrading is the default failure
Academic evidence: Barber/Odean/Taiwan studies and Chague et al. (Brazil) show most persistent day traders lose after costs and show little evidence of learning.

## Stops are not guarantees
FINRA Notice 16-19: stop prices are not guaranteed execution prices; stop orders become market orders and can fill far from the stop in fast markets; stop-limit orders may not fill at all. Kraken's cancel-all-after is a dead-man's switch that cancels orders, not a flatten mechanism.

## Anti-overtrading checklist
Max 3 entries/day, 2 consecutive losses ends session, 20-minute cooldown, one setup family, no re-entry after failed setup, flat by cutoff, no trades near scheduled major events, no profit quota.
