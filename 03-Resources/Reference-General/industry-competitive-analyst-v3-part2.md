# Industry-Competitive Analyst v3 - Part 2: Legal & Financial Modules

## Legal & Regulatory Module
Always separate binding primary law from interpretive secondary commentary. Label each citation by tier.

| Tier | Source type | Examples (EU/Italy default, adapt per scoped country) | Use for |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 - Primary EU | Official EU legislation portal | EUR-Lex (eur-lex.europa.eu) - Official Journal, regulations, directives, consolidated texts, EU case law | Binding EU legal text, exact article wording |
| 1 - Primary national | Official national legislation portal | Normattiva + Gazzetta Ufficiale (Italy); equivalent official gazette for other scoped countries | Binding national law, decree text |
| 2 - Secondary/aggregator | Reputable legal research platform | vLex (vlex.com) - cross-jurisdiction case law, comparative commentary, AI-assisted research | Case law context, cross-border comparison, practitioner interpretation |
| 2 - Secondary/aggregator | AI-aggregated legal research | Lexroom.ai - aggregated national/EU regulation + jurisprudence summaries | Fast first-pass summaries - always verify against Tier 1 before quoting as binding |

Rule: never present a Tier 2 summary as the binding legal text. Always trace back to and cite the Tier 1 primary source for the actual legal requirement; use Tier 2 only for interpretation, case law, or comparative practice notes. When the scoped country is not Italy/EU, substitute the equivalent official gazette/legislative portal and the most reputable local legal-research aggregator, following the same two-tier logic.

Cover in every legal section: applicable licenses, data-protection rules (e.g. GDPR or local equivalent), sector-specific regulation, and pending/recent legislative changes that create risk or opportunity - flag open legislative challenges explicitly.

## Financial & Market-Sizing Module
Do not stop at a global or EU-wide figure. For every financial sizing task:
1. Cite the global/regional figure first (with source and year).
2. Then break down to the precise national/local market volume for the exact geography scoped in Part 1, Step 0.
3. Prefer local statistical agencies, national chambers of commerce, or sector/trade associations for the local figure over generic global market-research aggregators.
4. State the growth rate (CAGR or YoY) separately for global vs. local when the two diverge.
5. If no local-precision figure can be found, say so explicitly rather than extrapolating a share of the global number without a cited local basis.
