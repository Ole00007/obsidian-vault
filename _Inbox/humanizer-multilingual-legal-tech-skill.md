# Multilingual Humanizer — Legal-Tech Copy

## Purpose

Use this skill to edit or translate legal-tech, B2B SaaS and professional-services copy in English, Italian and Russian. The result must read as if it were written or carefully edited by a native-speaking subject-matter professional.

The skill improves rhythm, clarity and credibility without inventing facts, changing legal meaning, weakening caveats or making promises the source does not make.

## Core rule

**Humanise the language; preserve the meaning.**

Keep intact:

- The factual meaning, scope, claims and limitations
- Headings, section order, numbered steps, links, calls to action and placeholders
- Privacy, security and legal disclaimers
- The distinction between what software does, what staff do and what a lawyer must decide
- Product names, approved terminology and any supplied glossary

Do not add made-up examples, outcomes, customer quotes, statistics, guarantees, legal advice or compliance claims.

## Language protocol

### English

Write polished international B2B English: direct, calm and specific. Prefer short active sentences where they improve clarity, but do not make every sentence equally short.

- Prefer: “The firm reviews the request before accepting it.”
- Avoid: “The request is subject to a comprehensive review process prior to acceptance.”
- Prefer: “A client can see the next approved step.”
- Avoid: “Clients are enabled to gain visibility into the next-stage progression.”

### Italian

Write natural Italian for a professional Italian audience. Use familiar legal-professional wording without turning the page into bureaucratic language. Respect Italian quotation marks and apostrophes. Use the formal *Lei* only where the brand voice calls for direct address; otherwise use neutral impersonal constructions.

- Prefer: “La richiesta passa prima a una verifica.”
- Avoid: “La richiesta viene sottoposta a un articolato processo di valutazione preliminare.”
- Prefer: “Lo studio indica con chiarezza il passaggio successivo.”
- Avoid: “Lo studio provvede a fornire indicazioni in merito alla successiva fase operativa.”

### Russian

Write fluent contemporary Russian for a professional audience. Avoid literal calques from English and excessive bureaucratic phrasing. Keep legal distinctions accurate; do not translate product concepts word for word if the result sounds unnatural.

- Prefer: “Запрос сначала проходит проверку.”
- Avoid: “Запрос подвергается осуществлению предварительного процесса ревью.”
- Prefer: “Клиент видит следующий согласованный шаг.”
- Avoid: “Клиенту предоставляется возможность получить видимость следующей стадии прогресса.”

## Humanisation rules

### 1. Vary the rhythm

Mix short sentences with longer explanatory ones. Break up long chains of abstract nouns. Do not force every paragraph into identical length or every list item into the same grammatical pattern.

### 2. Replace vague abstraction with concrete language

Name the actor and the action whenever the source permits it.

- Weak: “Visibility is provided around matter progression.”
- Better: “The client can see the latest approved update and the next step.”

### 3. Remove assistant-like phrasing

Delete or rewrite generic openings, inflated conclusions and filler.

Avoid constructions such as:

- “It is important to note that …”
- “In today’s rapidly evolving landscape …”
- “This powerful solution seamlessly enables …”
- “In order to …” when “to” is enough
- “Moreover”, “Furthermore” and “Additionally” repeated as paragraph starters
- “We hope this helps”, “great question” and other chatbot-style commentary

### 4. Preserve a professional voice

Human writing is not casual writing. For legal-tech copy, the tone should be assured, useful and restrained.

- Do not use hype: “revolutionary”, “game-changing”, “best-in-class”, “effortless”, unless these words are part of an approved brand claim.
- Do not simulate personal experience or a lawyer’s opinion.
- Do not use jokes in compliance, privacy, security or legal-disclaimer sections.

### 5. Respect legal and product boundaries

Use precise distinctions throughout.

| Do | Do not |
|---|---|
| Say that the firm or lawyer decides whether to accept a matter | Say or imply that software accepts a matter |
| Say that AI extracts or proposes information for review | Say that AI decides, interprets legal outcomes or gives legal advice |
| Say access is controlled or approved | Promise that a product is “fully secure” or “risk-free” |
| Preserve conditional language such as “may”, “where required” and “depends on the setup” | Turn conditional statements into unconditional promises |
| Keep “form submission does not create a lawyer-client relationship” where supplied | Shorten away or soften this disclaimer |

## Translation workflow

### Pass 1 — Meaning and structure

- Identify the audience, channel and required tone.
- Preserve the source’s headings, sequence, lists, links, emphasis and disclaimers.
- Create or apply an approved terminology list before translating repeated product and legal concepts.

### Pass 2 — Native-language rewrite

- Translate for meaning, not word order.
- Read every paragraph as a native professional would.
- Replace calques, rigid syntax, duplicated verbs and unexplained anglicisms.
- Retain the original degree of certainty. “May” must not become “will”; “approved” must not become “automatic”.

### Pass 3 — Human editorial pass

- Vary sentence length and transitions.
- Replace abstract wording with clear verbs and named actors.
- Remove formulaic AI patterns, filler and unjustified emphasis.
- Keep the voice consistent from the opening through the CTA and related links.

### Pass 4 — Safety and publishing check

- Confirm that links still point to the same targets.
- Check names, statuses, dates, product labels and repeated terms for consistency.
- Confirm that nothing in the edit creates legal, security, privacy or performance promises.
- Confirm the text works on a phone: short paragraphs, scannable lists and no overloaded sentences.

## Before-and-after examples

### Example 1 — Product explanation

**Draft**

> The platform provides enhanced visibility into the ongoing progression of the matter, enabling clients to remain informed in a seamless way.

**Humanised English**

> The platform lets clients see approved updates on their matter without having to call the firm for a routine status check.

**Italian**

> La piattaforma consente al cliente di consultare gli aggiornamenti approvati sulla propria pratica, senza dover chiamare lo studio per un normale aggiornamento sullo stato.

**Russian**

> Платформа позволяет клиенту видеть согласованные обновления по своему делу, не звоня в фирму по обычному вопросу о статусе.

### Example 2 — Legal boundary

**Draft**

> Our intelligent AI capabilities can provide guidance and make the intake process more efficient.

**Humanised English**

> AI can extract details from an enquiry and place them in fields for staff to review. It does not give legal advice or decide whether the firm accepts the matter.

**Italian**

> L’IA può estrarre i dati da una richiesta e inserirli in campi che il personale può verificare. Non fornisce consulenza legale e non decide se lo studio accetta la pratica.

**Russian**

> ИИ может извлечь сведения из обращения и перенести их в поля для проверки сотрудниками. Он не даёт юридических консультаций и не решает, примет ли фирма дело.

### Example 3 — Natural CTA

**Draft**

> Contact us today to unlock a seamless and comprehensive demonstration of our solution.

**Humanised English**

> Want to see how the workflow would work in your firm? Request a walkthrough and demo.

**Italian**

> Vuoi vedere come funzionerebbe il flusso nel tuo studio? Richiedi una panoramica e una demo.

**Russian**

> Хотите увидеть, как этот процесс будет работать в вашей фирме? Запросите обзор и демонстрацию.

## Final checklist

- Does every sentence have a clear purpose?
- Does the translation sound native when read aloud?
- Are repeated legal-tech terms translated consistently?
- Are links, headings, lists and caveats unchanged in function and placement?
- Did the rewrite avoid hype, vague claims and synthetic filler?
- Does the copy clearly separate system assistance from legal judgement?
- Does the text contain no invented claim or promise?
